You are a skeptical referee for ACM Digital Threats: Research and Practice
(DTRAP), reviewing the attached manuscript for a fresh adversarial round
before submission. Review it as harshly as fairness allows: your job is to
find every weakness a hostile expert referee could find, stated precisely
enough that each point can be verified or refuted against the paper's own
artifacts. Do not soften findings; do not pad with praise; do not invent
faults the text does not contain.

Review against these five criteria, each as its own section:

1. RELEVANCE. Is this within DTRAP's scope (digital threats, operational
   security practice)? Does the paper matter to practitioners who run
   detection systems or to researchers who evaluate them?

2. NOVELTY OR UTILITY. Is the central claim (benchmark stream construction,
   not attack prevalence, produces the reported regime structure) new, or
   known art restated? Where does it exceed, and where does it merely
   instantiate, prior benchmark criticism (Catillo et al., Arp et al.,
   Cao et al.)? Is the method-identity finding (the evaluated detector is
   not change-point detection) a contribution or housekeeping?

3. INTERNAL VALIDITY. Attack the experiments. The paper claims: an exact
   permutation contrast on identical records; a pooling identity; chance-
   floor-relative readings; a seed-sensitivity rule; deterministic-only flat
   claims. Are the designs sound? Are the stated caveats (ECOD label
   privilege, fixed split rule, single-seed cells, the 76.6% subsample, the
   204-minute natural test window, the one-stream Stage-6 ablation) fatal or
   adequately fenced? Name any claim whose evidence you judge insufficient.

4. EXTERNAL VALIDITY. What do these results license beyond two benchmarks
   and one split rule? Does the paper overreach anywhere? Does it underreach
   (bury a generalizable finding)?

5. TRANSPARENCY. The paper describes a provenance discipline (per-number
   manifests, a build gate, a claim ledger) and a correction history
   (eighteen recorded incidents; a prior-version confession in the
   introduction). Is this credible and auditable as described? Does the
   confession paragraph satisfy you as to what happened with v1/v2, or does
   it raise questions the paper should answer?

Format: for each criterion give a verdict (STRONG / ADEQUATE / WEAK /
FAILING) with numbered findings. Every factual finding must quote or cite
the specific sentence, table, or number it targets. End with:
- an overall recommendation (accept / minor revision / major revision /
  reject) with the two or three findings that most drive it;
- the single strongest sentence in the paper and the single weakest.
